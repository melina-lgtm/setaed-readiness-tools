R&D Readiness Analysis

An evidence-based readiness assessment defined by SETA-ED and adapted from the R&D portfolio practices of DARPA, ARPA-E, and other Advanced Research Project Agencies. Paste a grant proposal (or upload a .txt file) to receive a complete draft assessment against the three Inclusive R&D readiness frameworks — Prototype, People, and Process — with evidence quotes for every rubric question and an automated risk inventory based on the proposal.

Step 1. Paste the proposal text below. For .docx or .pdf files, open in Word/Preview, select all (⌘/Ctrl+A), and copy-paste here. For .txt files, use the upload button.
→ populates the textarea below
Step 2. Name the venture and run the analysis. The system performs two passes: a readiness scoring pass against all 57 rubric questions, then a risk identification pass that maps proposal content to the DARPA-style risk plan structure.
What the R&D Readiness Analysis returns:
  • Pass 1 — Readiness scoring. A score (No / Weak / Strong / Yes) for every PRL, PeoML, and ProML question, with a short evidence quote or paraphrase from the proposal supporting each score, and an overall summary of readiness across the three dimensions.
  • Pass 2 — Risk inventory. A populated DARPA-style risk plan: 4–7 risk categories surfaced from the proposal, each with the venture's stated short-term mitigation, their long-term mitigation plan, and a reviewer-facing confidence rating in the strength of that mitigation.
  • The whole assessment applied to a new venture in your portfolio — ready for you to review and refine.
Conservative scoring. The analysis is calibrated to score honestly — most ventures overstate their readiness, and the framework's value is in catching that. When the proposal mentions something only briefly or aspirationally, expect "Weak" rather than "Strong." Always re-read the evidence quotes and adjust scores where your judgment differs.
Methodology. The R&D Readiness Analysis is run by a language model executing the SETA-ED rubric. Proposal text is processed in real time and not retained beyond the venture's local record in this browser. The methodology — not the model — is the source of authority: the rubric, scoring anchors, and risk taxonomy were defined by Dr. Melina Uncapher (SETA-ED) drawing on AERDF and ARPA risk-management practice.

Scoring legend. For each rubric question, score the venture's evidence: No no evidence at all · Weak some evidence but not at the level the question asks · Strong clear evidence at the level the question asks · Yes documented evidence with citation/artifact. A level is "achieved" when every question at that level scores Strong or Yes and every level below is achieved.

The point for due diligence: ventures whose three readiness scores move together are the credible ones. A venture proposing a leap from PRL 3 to PRL 7 in one cycle (without People and Process advancing) is sandbagging or oversoiling. Use the rubric to surface this gap before committing funding.
